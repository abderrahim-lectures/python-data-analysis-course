# Book Notes: Deep Work (Cal Newport)

date: 2026-03-14
tags: books, productivity, focus

Central argument: the ability to focus without distraction on a cognitively
demanding task is becoming rare and, because it's rare, valuable. Shallow
work (email, quick admin) fills time but rarely produces anything hard to
replicate.

## Ideas worth keeping

- Schedule deep work like a meeting with yourself -- if it's not on the
  calendar, shallow work will always expand to fill the day.
- "Draining the shallows": cap shallow work at some fraction of the week
  rather than letting it be the default and deep work the leftover.
- Attention residue: switching tasks leaves a residue of the old task that
  degrades focus on the new one for longer than expected. Fewer, longer
  blocks beat frequent context switching.

## Where I disagree

Newport's schedule assumes a lot of control over your own calendar that
not everyone has (compare to the more social-systems view I wanted after
`atomic-habits.md`). Still useful as an ideal to negotiate toward.
