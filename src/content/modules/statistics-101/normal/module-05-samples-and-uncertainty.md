## Why This Matters

Every number you have computed so far came from data you had. Real statistics usually works the other way round: you have a sample and you want to say something about a population you never see. A survey of 400 voters, 30 quality-checked parts from a production line, one month of traffic on a website.

The sample mean you compute is an *estimate*. Run the survey again and you would get a slightly different number. The question this module answers is how much the answer would wobble, and how to state a range that is likely to contain the truth.

## What You'll Learn

- Explain the difference between a sample and a population, and between a statistic and a parameter
- Compute a standard error and say what it measures
- Use the normal curve's 68–95–99.7 rule to judge how far a value is from the mean
- Build a confidence interval for a mean by hand and interpret it correctly
