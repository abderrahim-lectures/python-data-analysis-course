## Why This Matters

You have a column of numbers. Maybe it is the price of 200 used bicycles, the time 40 students spent on a quiz, or the temperature recorded every hour for a month. The first question anyone asks is the same: *what does this column look like?* One number cannot answer that fully, but a single number that sits in the middle of the data answers it well enough to start a conversation.

That number is a **measure of centre**. The two you will meet most are the mean and the median, and the interesting part of this module is that they disagree. When they do, the disagreement is the finding, not a nuisance to be averaged away.

## What You'll Learn

- Compute a mean by hand and in Python, and say exactly what it represents
- Compute a median, and why sorting changes what the middle means
- Choose between them from the shape of the data, not from habit
- Explain to someone else why "the average" is not one thing
